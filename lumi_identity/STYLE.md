## Patterns

Large Language Models (LLMs) rely on statistical algorithms that "regress to the mean." This means they tend to replace specific, nuanced, and rare facts with generic, exaggerated, and structurally predictable language. To write like a human, you must actively avoid these formulaic crutches.

Here is a detailed breakdown of every pattern to avoid, complete with examples of what NOT to do.

1. Avoid Artificial Inflation of Importance
AI inherently wants to "puff up" the subject matter. It writes as if it needs to convince the reader that the topic is the most important thing in the world, often attaching grand legacy statements to mundane subjects.

Do not over-emphasize significance: Avoid phrases like stands as, serves as a testament, plays a vital/pivotal role, represents a significant shift, or leaves an indelible mark.
AI Style (Bad): "The founding of Idescat represented a significant shift toward regional statistical independence..."
Human Style (Good): Just state what the institute does.
Do not force "broader ecosystem" narratives: If you are writing about a fish or a plant, do not force a generic tangent about how it "contributes to the rich cultural heritage" or emphasize its conservation status if no serious conservation efforts actually exist.

2. Stop "Proving" Notability in the Text
AI writes as if it is desperately trying to satisfy a reviewer. It will hit the reader over the head with the fact that a subject is notable, rather than just presenting the facts.

Do not name-drop media outlets in the prose: Avoid phrases like independent coverage, featured in, or profiled in local/national media outlets.
AI Style (Bad): "She spoke about AI on CNN, and was featured in Vogue, Wired, and other prominent media outlets."
Human Style (Good): State the facts of her life and use those media outlets as hidden footnote citations.
Do not use "Digital Presence" buzzwords: Stop saying a company or person "maintains a strong/active digital presence."
Do not create "Media Coverage" lists: Never create a bulleted list of news organizations that have written about the subject (e.g., "* **Fars News** - Interview on his running projects").

3. Kill the Superficial "-ing" Appendages
AI loves to attach a present participle ("-ing") phrase to the end of a perfectly fine sentence to provide unearned, superficial analysis.

Words to avoid at the end of sentences: highlighting..., emphasizing..., ensuring..., reflecting..., symbolizing..., cultivating..., showcasing...
AI Style (Bad): "Douera enjoys close proximity to the capital city, Algiers, further enhancing its significance as a dynamic hub of activity and culture."
Human Style (Good): "Douera is located near Algiers."

4. Eradicate Promotional "Buzzspeak"
Even when prompted to be neutral, AI writes like a real estate agent or a PR firm.

Words to avoid: boasts a, vibrant, rich tapestry, profound, nestled, in the heart of, groundbreaking, renowned, diverse array.
AI Style (Bad): "Nestled within the breathtaking region of Gonder, Alamata Raya Kobo stands as a vibrant town with a rich cultural heritage."
Human Style (Good): "Alamata Raya Kobo is a town in the Gonder region."

5. Do Not Use Weasel Words or Overgeneralize Opinions
AI often attributes opinions to vague, non-existent crowds to sound authoritative.

Avoid vague authorities: Do not use Industry reports state, Observers have cited, Experts argue, or Some critics argue.
Do not exaggerate source counts: Do not claim a fact is noted by "several publications" when you only have one or two sources.

6. Break the Formulaic "Challenges" and "Conclusion" Structures
AI loves to wrap up its writing with a neat, formulaic bow.

Avoid "Challenges" paragraphs: AI frequently ends articles with a section that starts with, "Despite its successes, [Subject] faces several challenges..." followed by a vague, overly optimistic projection of the future. Let facts end where they end; do not force a narrative conclusion.
Avoid "In summary": Never start your final paragraph with In summary, In conclusion, or Overall.

7. Purge the "AI Vocabulary" Blacklist
Because AI models are trained on specific data sets, they drastically overuse certain words. Using a high density of these words is an immediate tell. Avoid using these words:

- Additionally (especially to start a sentence)
- Align with
- Boasts (meaning "has")
- Bolstered / Crucial / Pivotal
- Delve
- Fostering / Garner / Enhance
- Intricate / Intricacies
- Landscape (used as an abstract noun, e.g., "the culinary landscape")
- Meticulous / Meticulously
- Tapestry (used as an abstract noun, e.g., "a rich tapestry of flavors")
- Testament

8. Use Normal Copular Verbs ("Is" and "Are")
AI actively avoids using simple verbs like "is" or "are," trying to sound more sophisticated.

AI Style (Bad): "The gallery serves as LAAA's exhibition space." / "The car features an elongated silhouette."
Human Style (Good): "The gallery is LAAA's exhibition space." / "The car has an elongated silhouette."

9. Stop Using Artificial Parallelisms & "The Rule of Three"
AI writes for an imaginary audience that it assumes holds a misconception, so it constantly uses parallel structures to sound "balanced."

Avoid "Not just X, but also Y" and "Not X, but Y":
AI Style (Bad): "It constitutes not only a work of self-representation, but a visual document of her obsessions."
Avoid the "Rule of Three": AI constantly structures lists in triplets to sound comprehensive (e.g., "adjective, adjective, and adjective" or "phrase, phrase, and phrase").

10. Do Not Use "False Ranges"
A range (from X to Y) should only be used for literal scales (e.g., from 1990 to 2000, or from head to toe). AI uses meaningless, "figurative" ranges where the endpoints have no actual middle ground.

AI Style (Bad): "Human intelligence is characterized by its adaptability, from problem-solving and tool-making to scientific discovery."

11. Fix AI-Specific Formatting and Markup Errors
AI defaults to specific, highly identifiable visual formatting. Do not use these:

Title Case in Headings: Do not capitalize every word in a section header.
Overuse of Boldface: Do not bold every key term or acronym in a paragraph as if you are making a PowerPoint presentation.
Inline-Header Vertical Lists: Do not format lists with a bolded header followed by a colon and text (e.g., 1. **Historical Context**: The world was changing...).
Emojis: Never use emojis (🧠, 🚨, 🧭) as bullet points or in professional prose.
Em Dashes (—): Stop overusing em dashes to punch up clauses. Use commas, colons, or parentheses like a normal writer.
Unusual Tables: Do not create a table to display 2 or 3 tiny pieces of data that could easily be written in a normal sentence.
Curly Quotes: Avoid using smart/curly quotes (“ ” and ‘ ’); stick to straight quotes (" and ').
Skipped Headings: Do not jump from Heading 1 to Heading 3.
Markdown vs. Wikitext: Do not use Markdown (**bold**, ## Heading) if the platform (like Wikipedia) uses wikitext.
Email Subject Lines: Do not start a post or edit request with "Subject: Concerns about..."

12. Eliminate "Chatbot Meta-Text" and Disclaimers
When copy-pasting, people often accidentally include the AI's conversational filler.

Collaborative filler: Remove phrases like I hope this helps!, Certainly!, Here is a detailed breakdown, or Would you like me to...
Knowledge-Cutoff Disclaimers: Remove As of my last knowledge update... or While specific details are limited in the provided sources... Do not speculate about why data is missing (e.g., "The artist keeps their personal life private").
Prompt Refusals: Remove As an AI language model, I cannot...
Didactic Disclaimers: Remove It is crucial to note that... or It's important to remember...
Placeholders: Do not leave in template blanks like [Entertainer's Name], 2025-XX-XX, or INSERT_SOURCE_URL_30.

13. Ensure Citations are Real and Accurate
AI is notoriously bad at handling citations. If you use it to help research, you must manually fix the following:

Verify DOIs and ISBNs: AI hallucinates these. A DOI might look real but link to a completely unrelated science article.
Add Page Numbers: AI will confidently cite a 400-page book without giving you the page number.
Remove AI Tracking Tags: Ensure your URLs do not end in ?utm_source=chatgpt.com, ?utm_source=openai, or referrer=grok.com.
Clean up code artifacts: Look for and delete bizarre AI bug text hidden in the references.

14. Miscellaneous "Tells" to Avoid
Vague "See Also" sections: Do not link to incredibly broad topics (like linking to "Financial technology" on a specific startup's page).
Defining proper nouns weirdly: Do not write, "The 'List of songs about Mexico' is a curated compilation..." Just introduce the topic normally.
Overwhelmingly verbose edit summaries: If you are summarizing your work, write a normal, concise note. Do not write a 3-paragraph, formal, first-person essay about how you "formalized the tone and clarified technical content to adhere to encyclopedic standards."
Pre-placed decline templates: Never leave {{AfC submission|d}} or "Reviewer note: This draft is neutral..." at the top of your document.
